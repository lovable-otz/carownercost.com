Car Sales Tax by State (2026)

Car sales tax by state ranges from 0% in Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon to a 10.13% combined state and average local rate in Louisiana, according to the Tax Foundation’s 2026 midyear update (July 6, 2026). On a $30,000 car, that is $0 to $3,039 before vehicle-specific rules.

Which states charge the most sales tax on cars?

Louisiana, Tennessee, Washington, Arkansas and Alabama have the highest combined general sales tax rates in the 2026 midyear data. All five are above 9.4% once the average local rate is added, which means more than $2,800 on a $30,000 car where the general rate applies to vehicles.

States reach the top in two ways. Tennessee, Washington and Arkansas pair a high state rate with local add-ons. Louisiana and Alabama have lower state rates, but their local rates average above 5%.

On the state rate alone, California is highest at 7.25%, and Indiana, Mississippi, Rhode Island and Tennessee tie at 7%, per the Tax Foundation. California’s combined 9.03% ranks seventh in the table.

These are averages. Local rates differ from one city or county to the next, so your rate can be higher or lower than the figure shown. Enter your own local rate in the car sales tax calculator for a closer estimate.

Why your vehicle tax may differ from this table

This table shows general sales tax rates, and several states tax vehicles under separate rules: a different rate, a cap, a separate title or highway tax, or their own treatment of trade-ins. What you actually pay can be lower or higher than the table, so confirm with your state DMV before you buy.

Examples from official state sources:

  • North Carolina collects a highway use tax instead of sales tax when a title is transferred (NCDMV). An NCDOT brief describes it as 3% of the price less a dealer trade-in, so the table’s 7.1% general rate is not what a car buyer there typically pays.
  • Oregon shows 0% here, but charges a 0.5% vehicle privilege or use tax on vehicles driven 7,500 miles or less (Oregon Department of Revenue).
  • California taxes the full price even when you trade in a car (CDTFA), at the rate for the address where you register it (CDTFA).
  • Ohio lets a trade-in reduce the taxable price only on a new vehicle (Ohio Department of Taxation).

Two more reasons for a gap:

  • Local rates. The table uses an average local rate. In New Jersey, the Tax Foundation notes that some cities are in Urban Enterprise Zones, where qualifying sellers collect half the statewide rate.
  • Fees. Title, registration and plate fees are not in this table and are charged separately.

Frequently asked questions

Which state has the lowest car sales tax?

Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no state or local general sales tax, so the table shows 0%. Alaska has no state sales tax, but its local taxes average 1.82%. These states can still charge vehicle fees or taxes; Oregon, for example, has a 0.5% tax on vehicles driven 7,500 miles or less.

How much sales tax is on a $30,000 car?

At 2026 combined general rates, anywhere from $0 in the four states with no sales tax to $3,039 in Louisiana. Texas comes to $2,460 at 8.2%, Ohio to $2,187 at 7.29% and California to $2,709 at 9.03%, before any vehicle-specific rule. Your local rate and your state’s trade-in treatment change the result.

Can I avoid sales tax by buying a car in another state?

Often not, because states can collect use tax where the car is registered. California, for example, collects use tax at registration on a car bought without paying California sales tax, at the rate for your registration address, with a possible credit for tax paid to another state. Check your home state’s DMV before shopping across a border.
