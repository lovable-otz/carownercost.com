Where the numbers come from

Every figure on Car Owner Cost comes from a named public source. Gas prices are the U.S. Energy Information Administration’s weekly regular gasoline averages (currently the week of September 14, 2026), refreshed after each Tuesday release. Home electricity prices in the EV tool are EIA’s monthly state residential averages (currently June 2026), refreshed monthly. Sales tax rates are the Tax Foundation’s state and average local rates (2026 midyear update), refreshed when a new edition is published, at least once a year. Loan rates, depreciation and your share of income are your own inputs, labeled as assumptions. The site is maintained by Car Owner Cost, and every formula is written out on the methodology page.

Common questions

How do I tell whether refinancing my car loan is worth it?

Compare the total of your remaining payments with the total of the new loan’s payments plus fees. If the new total is lower, refinancing saves money on those terms. More in Auto Refinance Calculator.

Is it a good idea to refinance an auto loan?

It can be when the new APR is lower, the term doesn’t stretch much and fees are modest. It may not be if your loan is nearly paid off, a prepayment penalty applies or the lower payment only comes from a longer term. More in Auto Refinance Calculator.

How do you calculate a refinance for an auto loan?

Work out the monthly payment on your remaining balance at the current APR and months left, and again at the new APR and term. Multiply each payment by its number of months, add fees to the new side, and subtract. More in Auto Refinance Calculator.

What is a good rate for refinancing a car?

There is no single good rate; offers depend on your credit, the car and the lender. One benchmark: commercial banks averaged 7.14% on 60-month new-car loans in the second quarter of 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19). More in Auto Refinance Calculator.

How much should I spend on a car if I make $70,000 a year?

At a 10% payment share, $70,000 a year supports about $583 a month. With a 6% APR over 48 months and $4,000 down, that points to a car price of about $28,839 including financed tax and fees. More in Car Affordability Calculator.

How much should I spend on a car if I make $100,000 a year?

Using the same example assumptions (10% of gross income, 6% APR, 48 months, $4,000 down), $100,000 a year supports a payment of about $833 and a car price near $39,484. More in Car Affordability Calculator.
