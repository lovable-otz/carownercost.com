MPG Calculator

An MPG calculator divides the miles you drove by the gallons it took to refill the tank. Drive 320 miles, refill with 11.5 gallons, and you got 27.83 MPG, close to the 27.2 MPG average for new model-year 2024 vehicles in EPA’s Automotive Trends report (February 19, 2026).

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Car Owner Cost

How do I calculate my MPG?

Fill the tank, reset the trip meter, drive as usual, then fill up again. Divide the miles on the trip meter by the gallons the second fill took. That is your real fuel economy for that tank, and it usually says more about your car than the label does.

Worked example: 320 miles ÷ 11.5 gallons = 27.83 MPG. At $4.319 a gallon, that is about 15.5 cents a mile, or 8.45 liters per 100 km.

  1. Fill until the pump clicks off, without topping off.
  2. Write down the trip miles before the next fill.
  3. Take the gallons from the receipt, not the fuel gauge.
  4. For a steadier figure, add up the miles and gallons across several tanks and divide the totals.

Cost per mile is the price per gallon divided by MPG. Carry your own MPG into the gas calculator to price a trip with a number that reflects how you drive.

Why is my real MPG lower than the sticker?

The sticker comes from standardized lab tests, and everyday driving rarely matches them. EPA runs vehicles on a dynamometer through five test cycles, including a high-speed test, a hot test at 95°F with the air conditioning on and a cold test at 20°F, then weights the results into the label figure (EPA, updated July 1, 2026).

fueleconomy.gov lists five reasons your result can differ (Your Mileage Will Vary):

  • How and where you drive
  • Vehicle condition and maintenance
  • Fuel variations
  • Differences between individual vehicles of the same model
  • Engine break-in on a new car

Driving habits are the part you control most directly. The same site says aggressive driving can lower mileage by roughly 15% to 30% at highway speeds and 10% to 40% in stop-and-go traffic, and that an extra 100 pounds in the car could cost about 1%. If your measured MPG sits far below the label tank after tank, consider having the car checked.

How to convert MPG to L/100 km

Divide 235.215 by the MPG figure to get liters per 100 kilometers. The constant combines the U.S. gallon (3.785 liters) and the mile (1.609 km). So 27.83 MPG equals 8.45 L/100 km. The scale runs the opposite way to MPG: a lower L/100 km figure means better fuel economy.

Run it in reverse (235.215 ÷ L/100 km) to turn a Canadian or European figure into U.S. MPG. MPG figures from the UK use the larger imperial gallon, so they are not the same unit and will not match.

Frequently asked questions

Is 20 MPG a lot?

No, 20 MPG is below average for a new vehicle. EPA’s Automotive Trends report (February 2026) puts the model-year 2024 new-vehicle average at a record 27.2 MPG, and truck SUVs at 25.7 MPG. At $4.319 a gallon, 20 MPG costs about 21.6 cents a mile, against 14.4 cents at 30 MPG.

Is 42 MPG a lot?

Yes, 42 MPG is well above the 27.2 MPG average EPA reported for new model-year 2024 vehicles in February 2026. At this week’s U.S. average of $4.319 a gallon, it works out to about 10.3 cents a mile, or 5.60 liters per 100 km.

Is 300 miles per gallon good?

That figure is far beyond the 27.2 MPG new-vehicle average EPA reports, so it usually describes miles on one tank rather than per gallon. To turn it into MPG, divide 300 by the gallons needed to refill: 300 miles on 10 gallons is 30 MPG, and on 12 gallons it is 25 MPG.
