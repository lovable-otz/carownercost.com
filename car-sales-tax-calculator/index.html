Car Sales Tax Calculator

A car sales tax calculator multiplies the taxable price by your state and local rate. In California, a $30,000 car at the 7.25% state rate plus the 1.78% average local rate comes to $2,709, using the Tax Foundation’s 2026 midyear rates (July 6, 2026). Your state’s vehicle rules can change that figure.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Car Owner Cost

How much is sales tax on a car?

Sales tax on a car is usually the taxable price times the combined state and local rate. In the 2026 midyear data, combined general rates run from 0% in four states to 10.13% in Louisiana (Tax Foundation). On a $30,000 car, that is $0 to $3,039 before any vehicle-specific rule.

How to use the tool:

  1. Pick your state. It loads the state rate and the average local rate.
  2. Enter the price. If you know your city or county rate, type it in place of the average.
  3. Enter any trade-in, and tick the box only if your state taxes the price minus the trade-in.

Examples at $30,000 with no trade-in: Texas at 8.2% is $2,460, Ohio at 7.29% is $2,187 and California at 9.03% is $2,709. The car sales tax by state table lists all 50 states and DC.

Treat the result as an estimate. States add title and registration fees, and some cap the tax or replace sales tax with a separate vehicle tax, so confirm the figure with your state DMV or revenue department. To see how the tax changes a monthly payment, use the car payment calculator.

Do you pay sales tax on a trade-in?

It depends on the state. Some states tax only the price minus your trade-in allowance, some tax the full price, and some give the credit only on certain purchases. Check your state’s rule before you tick the trade-in box in the calculator.

Three examples from official sources:

  • California: no credit. CDTFA tells dealers they “cannot deduct the allowance for the trade-in”; a $20,000 car with a $4,000 trade-in is taxed on $20,000 (CDTFA).
  • Ohio: new vehicles only. A trade-in lowers the taxable price when you buy a new motor vehicle, not a used one, according to the Ohio Department of Taxation.
  • North Carolina: credit at dealers. The 3% highway use tax is charged on the purchase price less any trade-in value when you buy from a dealership, per an NCDOT issue brief.

The difference is real money. A $30,000 California purchase with a $5,000 trade-in is taxed on the full $30,000: $2,709 at the average rate. With a credit, the same numbers would give $2,257.50. For California, leave the box unticked.

Which states have no sales tax?

Five states have no statewide sales tax: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon, according to the Tax Foundation’s 2026 midyear update. Of those, only Alaska lets local governments add one, and its local rates average 1.82%. The calculator shows the other four at 0%.

No sales tax does not mean no tax on a car. Oregon charges a vehicle privilege tax (collected by Oregon dealers) and a vehicle use tax (on cars bought outside Oregon) of 0.5% of the retail price on vehicles driven 7,500 miles or less, according to the Oregon Department of Revenue. On a new $30,000 car, that is $150.

States also charge title and registration fees, which this calculator does not include. Before buying in any of these states, check the state DMV for what applies to your purchase.

Where you pay: the state you register in

Vehicle tax often follows the place you register the car, not the place you buy it. If you buy out of state and bring the car home, your home state can collect use tax when you register, sometimes with a credit for tax you already paid elsewhere.

California’s rules show how this works (CDTFA purchaser guide):

  • Use tax generally applies when sales tax is not paid to a California dealer.
  • You usually pay it when you register the vehicle with the DMV.
  • The rate is the sales tax rate for the address where you register the vehicle.
  • If you paid tax to another state, you may be able to claim a credit for it.

Oregon’s 0.5% vehicle use tax works the same way in reverse: it applies to Oregon buyers who purchase from dealers outside the state.

So shopping across the border to a lower-tax state may not lower your tax bill. In the calculator, pick the state where the car will be registered, and enter the local rate for the address on the registration.

Frequently asked questions

How much is NC vehicle tax?

North Carolina charges a highway use tax instead of sales tax when a title is transferred, according to NCDMV. An NCDOT issue brief describes it as a one-time 3% of the purchase price, less any trade-in at a dealership, so a $30,000 car with no trade-in owes $900. To estimate it, enter 3% as the tax rate in the car payment calculator. Vehicles also owe county property tax, which NCDMV collects.

How much is sales tax on a $20,000 car in Florida?

Florida’s 6% state rate comes to $1,200 on a $20,000 car. Counties add a discretionary surtax (the 2026 midyear average local rate is 0.98%), but Florida applies it to vehicles under its own rules, so the simple combined rate may not match your bill. Check with the Florida Department of Revenue or your county tax collector for the exact total.

How much is car sales tax in Ohio?

Ohio’s state rate is 5.75% and its average local rate is 1.54%, for 7.29% combined (Tax Foundation, 2026 midyear). On a $30,000 car, that is about $2,187. The Ohio Department of Taxation says a trade-in lowers the taxable price only when you buy a new vehicle, so leave the trade-in box unticked for a used car.

How do you calculate taxes on a car?

Start with the price, subtract the trade-in if your state allows it, then multiply by the state rate plus your local rate. For a $30,000 California car with no trade-in, $30,000 × 9.03% = $2,709. Add title and registration fees separately, and check whether your state uses a separate vehicle tax instead.
