Gas Prices This Week by Region

Gas prices this week average $4.319 a gallon for regular in the U.S., according to the EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update for the week of September 14, 2026 (released September 15, 2026). Of EIA’s regions, the Gulf Coast is lowest at $3.852 and the West Coast highest at $5.467.

What is the national average gas price?

The national average for regular gasoline was $4.319 a gallon in the week of September 14, 2026. That was 16.2 cents higher than a week earlier and $1.151 higher than a year earlier, according to EIA’s September 15, 2026 release.

EIA builds the figure from a sample of about 1,000 retail stations, recording the self-serve cash price with taxes included as of 8:00 a.m. local time each Monday. Results are published around 10:00 a.m. ET on Tuesday (EIA survey methods). The next release is scheduled for September 22, 2026.

The regions in the table are Petroleum Administration for Defense Districts (PADDs). According to EIA, the five districts group the 50 states and DC; they were first used to ration gasoline in World War II and were set up in their current form under the Defense Production Act of 1950. PADD 1, the East Coast, is split into New England, Central Atlantic and Lower Atlantic. PADD 2 is the Midwest, PADD 3 the Gulf Coast, PADD 4 the Rocky Mountain region and PADD 5 the West Coast. The states listed below the regions are the ones EIA reports separately.

The 300-mile column assumes 25 MPG. For your own car and route, use the gas calculator.

Why are West Coast gas prices higher?

This week the West Coast averaged $5.467, which is $1.148 above the national average. California, at $5.827, is the highest figure in the table, and EIA’s explanation centers on that state:

  • Relatively few refineries make California’s own gasoline blend.
  • California’s reformulated gasoline program is stricter than the federal one.
  • California’s gasoline tax is higher than most states’.
  • When refineries have problems, replacement supply from other regions or abroad can take a relatively long time to arrive.

The rest of the West Coast was also above the national average this week, at $5.026. EIA’s general reasons for regional gaps apply there too: prices tend to rise the farther gasoline travels from its supply source, refinery and pipeline outages tighten supply, and stations with fewer competitors can charge more.

The gap is not new. In EIA’s 2025 annual averages, the West Coast paid $4.094 a gallon against $2.677 on the Gulf Coast. On a 600-mile round trip at 30 MPG, this week’s West Coast average costs $109.34, against $77.04 on the Gulf Coast.

Frequently asked questions

When are gas prices updated?

EIA collects prices every Monday and publishes them around 10:00 a.m. ET on Tuesday, or on Wednesday after a government holiday. The table on this page shows the week its prices come from; this edition covers the week of September 14, 2026, and the next EIA release is scheduled for September 22, 2026.

Which state has the cheapest gas this week?

Of the nine states EIA reports separately, Texas was lowest at $3.806 in the week of September 14, 2026, and California highest at $5.827. EIA does not publish weekly averages for the other states, so this page cannot rank them. Among regions, the Gulf Coast was lowest at $3.852.

Is the EIA average what I will pay at the pump?

Not exactly. EIA’s figure averages self-serve cash prices, taxes included, from a sample of about 1,000 stations, and a single station can sit above or below it. The average works well for planning a trip; for a specific fill-up, enter the price on the pump in the gas calculator.
