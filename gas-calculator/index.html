Gas Cost Calculator

A gas calculator divides your trip miles by your car’s MPG and multiplies by the price per gallon. At this week’s U.S. average of $4.319 for regular (EIA, week of September 14, 2026), a 600-mile round trip in a 30 MPG car uses 20 gallons and costs $86.38.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Car Owner Cost

How do you calculate gas cost for a trip?

Trip gas cost = miles driven ÷ your car’s MPG × price per gallon. Double the one-way distance for a round trip. The answer is the fuel you burn on the drive, not what your tank holds, so it works whether you leave with a full tank or a quarter tank.

Worked example with the tool’s defaults: 300 miles each way is 600 miles. At 30 MPG that takes 20 gallons. At the EIA U.S. average of $4.319 a gallon, the trip costs $86.38. One way only, it would be $43.19.

Three inputs move the answer most:

  • MPG. Use the highway figure from your car’s label, or measure your own with the MPG calculator. The same 600 miles cost $103.66 at 25 MPG and $129.57 at 20 MPG.
  • Price. Pick the EIA area you will drive through, or type the price you see at the pump.
  • Distance. Map apps give road miles; allow a little extra for detours and parking.

Treat the result as an estimate. EPA ratings come from standardized tests, and fueleconomy.gov points out that how you drive, the car’s condition and the fuel itself all change the MPG you really get.

What is the average gas price this week?

The U.S. average for regular gasoline was $4.319 a gallon in the week of September 14, 2026, according to the EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update released September 15, 2026. That was 16.2 cents higher than the week before and $1.151 higher than a year earlier.

Regional averages differ widely. In the same release the Gulf Coast averaged $3.852 and the West Coast $5.467, with California at $5.827 and Texas at $3.806. On a 600-mile round trip at 30 MPG, that is the gap between $77.04 on the Gulf Coast and $109.34 on the West Coast.

EIA surveys a sample of about 1,000 stations every Monday as of 8:00 a.m. local time, recording the self-serve cash price with taxes included, and publishes on Tuesday (EIA survey methods). The tool uses the latest release, and the full regional table is on the gas prices this week page.

A single station can sit well above or below its regional average, so enter your own price when you know it.

How to split gas money fairly

The simplest fair split divides the total fuel cost by the number of people in the car. The calculator does this for you: the 600-mile, $86.38 example comes to $28.79 each for three people, or $43.19 each for two.

Equal shares work when everyone rides the whole way. When they don’t, consider one of these approaches:

  • By distance. Divide each rider’s miles by the MPG and multiply by the price. Someone who joins for the last 150 miles of a 300-mile leg uses 5 gallons’ worth at 30 MPG, about $21.60 at $4.319.
  • Driver in or out. Groups differ on whether the car’s owner pays a share. Agree on it before you leave.
  • Beyond fuel. Tolls and parking are easy to add on top. Wear on the car is harder to price, so settle it upfront if it matters to the owner.

Agree on the price too: the EIA average for the area, or the actual receipts from each fill-up. Real receipts end any debate about which number to use.

How much does speed change fuel use?

Speed matters most above about 50 mph. The Department of Energy and EPA’s fueleconomy.gov says gas mileage “usually decreases rapidly at speeds above 50 mph,” and compares each extra 5 mph over 50 to paying an additional $0.29 per gallon.

The same page lists other habits that cost fuel:

  • Aggressive driving (speeding, rapid acceleration and hard braking) can lower mileage by roughly 15% to 30% at highway speeds and 10% to 40% in stop-and-go traffic.
  • Idling can burn a quarter to a half gallon an hour, depending on engine size and air conditioner use.
  • An extra 100 pounds in the vehicle could cut MPG by about 1%.
  • A rooftop cargo box can reduce fuel economy by around 6% to 17% on the highway and 10% to 25% at Interstate speeds.

To see what a heavier foot costs on your trip, lower the MPG in the calculator. Dropping from 30 to 25 MPG on the 600-mile example raises the fuel bill from $86.38 to $103.66.

Frequently asked questions

How much gas will I use on a 600-mile trip?

Divide the distance by your MPG. At 30 MPG, 600 miles takes 20 gallons; at 25 MPG, 24 gallons; at 20 MPG, 30 gallons. Multiply by the price per gallon for the cost: 20 gallons at this week’s U.S. average of $4.319 is $86.38. Your car’s real MPG on the day sets the final number, so treat these as estimates.

What does gas cost per mile?

Divide the price per gallon by your MPG. At $4.319 a gallon and 30 MPG, each mile costs about 14.4 cents; at 20 MPG it rises to about 21.6 cents. The MPG calculator works this out from a real fill-up, which usually reflects your own driving better than the window-sticker figure.

Is the regional average or my local pump price better for a trip estimate?

Your local pump price is better when you know it, because a station can sit well above or below its region’s average. The EIA regional figures are a good stand-in when you are planning a route through several states or have not seen prices along the way yet. The tool accepts either one.
