Car Loan Payoff Calculator

A car loan payoff calculator shows how much time and interest extra payments save. A $20,000 balance at 7% paid at $400 a month takes 60 months and $3,715.34 of interest; adding $100 a month clears it in 46 months with $2,841.23 of interest. First confirm your contract has no prepayment penalty (CFPB).

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Car Owner Cost

How do extra payments shorten a car loan?

Each month, interest is charged on the balance you still owe. Money you pay above the scheduled amount goes straight to principal, as long as your lender applies it that way, so the next month’s interest is smaller and more of every later payment reduces the balance. The effect compounds over the loan.

The calculator runs the loan month by month, with and without the extra amount. Using the defaults:

PaymentMonths to pay offTotal interest
$400 a month60$3,715.34
$500 a month ($100 extra)46$2,841.23
Difference14 months sooner$874.11 saved

With the extra $100, the balance falls to about $15,250 after 12 months, $10,156 after 24 and $4,693 after 36. The calculator prints this year-by-year balance under the result.

Ask your lender how to mark extra money as a principal payment. Confirm how the extra amount shows up on your next statement.

To model a lump sum instead, such as a tax refund, subtract it from the balance and rerun the numbers.

Should I pay off my car loan early?

Paying early saves interest, and the saving is larger when your APR is high and many months remain. Whether it is the best use of your cash is a personal decision. For scale, banks averaged 7.14% on 60-month new-car loans in the second quarter of 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19, September 8, 2026); every dollar of principal paid early avoids interest at your loan’s rate.

Points worth weighing:

  • Your rate. A higher APR makes early payoff save more per dollar.
  • Emergency savings. Money sent to the lender is hard to get back if something goes wrong.
  • Other debts. Consider where a higher interest rate is costing you more.
  • Penalties. A prepayment penalty can shrink or erase the saving.

If the loan’s rate is high, refinancing may save more than extra payments; compare both with the auto refinance calculator. Early payoff can also matter for add-on products bought with the loan, such as gap coverage or a service contract; ask the provider whether any unused portion is refundable.

What is a prepayment penalty?

A prepayment penalty is a fee some lenders charge when you pay off a loan early, because they lose interest they expected to earn. Some states prohibit these penalties for certain loans (CFPB, reviewed January 30, 2024). Check your contract and state law before you send extra money.

Where to look: your Truth-in-Lending disclosure must say whether you can prepay without a penalty (CFPB, reviewed March 8, 2024). The loan contract gives the details.

If you are still shopping, the CFPB suggests asking the lender or dealer directly whether the contract has a prepayment penalty, and notes you can negotiate to have it removed or look for a different loan (CFPB).

If your loan does carry one, compare the penalty with the interest the calculator says you would save. A $200 penalty against $874 of saved interest still leaves you ahead; against $150 of saved interest it does not.

Frequently asked questions

Is it smart to pay off a car loan early?

It saves interest if your lender applies extra money to principal and charges no prepayment penalty. In the default example, $100 extra a month on $20,000 at 7% saves $874.11 and 14 months. Consider your emergency savings and any higher-rate debts before deciding where extra cash goes.

How can I pay off a 6-year car loan in 3 years?

Raise the payment to what a 36-month loan would require. On $30,000 at 7%, the 72-month payment is about $511.47; adding roughly $415 a month (about $926.31 total) clears it in about three years and cuts interest from about $6,826 to about $3,347. Enter your own balance and rate to find your number.

How much is the monthly payment for a $30,000 car?

Financing $30,000 at 7.14% for 60 months, the bank average for the second quarter of 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19), costs about $596.02 a month and $5,761.17 in interest. Your rate, term, tax and down payment change this; the car payment calculator includes all of them.
