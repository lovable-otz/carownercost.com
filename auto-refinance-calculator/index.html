Auto Refinance Calculator

An auto refinance calculator compares what your current loan will cost with a new offer, after fees. Refinancing $18,000 from 9% to 5.5% over 48 months cuts the payment from $447.93 to $418.62 and saves about $1,107 after a $300 fee. Paying off the old loan can trigger a prepayment penalty (CFPB).

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Car Owner Cost

When does refinancing a car loan make sense?

Refinancing tends to pay off when the new APR is meaningfully lower, the new term is no longer than the months you have left, and fees are small next to the interest saved. The calculator adds up every remaining payment on both loans, subtracts the fees and shows the difference.

The default example:

Balance$18,000$18,000
APR9%5.5%
Months48 left48
Monthly payment$447.93$418.62
Upfront feesnone$300

Net saving: about $1,107.08 over the four years. Rates in the example are illustrations, not offers.

A few questions to ask first. Has your credit improved since you took the original loan? Are market rates lower? For context, banks averaged 7.14% on 60-month new-car loans in the second quarter of 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19, September 8, 2026). And does your current contract carry a prepayment penalty? Enter any penalty as part of the refinance fees so the comparison stays honest.

The CFPB suggests comparing offers on the loan amount, APR, length and monthly payment together (CFPB, reviewed January 30, 2024), which is exactly the side-by-side this tool builds.

How can a lower rate cost more?

A lower rate can still cost more when the new loan runs much longer, because you pay interest for more months. Fees and add-ons rolled into the new loan push the same way. That is why the calculator compares total cost, not just the monthly payment.

Take the same $18,000 balance at 9% with 48 months left. A refinance at 7% stretched to 72 months looks attractive at first:

Keep current loanRefinance at 7%, 72 months
Monthly payment$447.93$306.88
Months4872
Total of payments$21,500.68$22,095.51
Fees$0$300

The payment drops by about $141, yet you pay about $894.84 more overall once the fee is included. The tool shows a warning when this happens.

The CFPB makes the same point for new loans: a longer term can mean smaller payments while you pay more in interest (CFPB), and it raises the chance of owing more than the car is worth (CFPB, reviewed August 28, 2023). If a lower payment is the goal, that can still be a reasonable choice; just make it knowing the total cost. To pay the new loan down faster later, try the car loan payoff calculator.

What fees come with refinancing?

Common costs are a prepayment penalty on the old loan, lender fees on the new one, and state title or lien-transfer charges. The CFPB warns that paying off your old loan to refinance can bring a fee if your contract has a prepayment penalty (CFPB, reviewed January 30, 2024).

  • Prepayment penalty. Check your contract and your state law; some states prohibit these penalties for certain loans (CFPB).
  • Lender fees. Origination and document fees are among the items the CFPB lists as negotiable (CFPB).
  • Government fees. Title and registration charges set by your state are not negotiable.
  • Add-on products. Gap coverage, extended warranties and similar extras are optional (FTC, updated July 2022).

Before signing, read the new Truth-in-Lending disclosure. It lists the APR, finance charge, amount financed and total of payments (CFPB, reviewed March 8, 2024). If the amount financed is higher than your payoff balance, find out what was added. Frequently asked questions

How do I tell whether refinancing my car loan is worth it?

Compare the total of your remaining payments with the total of the new loan’s payments plus fees. If the new total is lower, refinancing saves money on those terms. In the default example, moving $18,000 from 9% to 5.5% over the same 48 months saves about $1,107 after a $300 fee.

Is it a good idea to refinance an auto loan?

It can be when the new APR is lower, the term doesn’t stretch much and fees are modest. It may not be if your loan is nearly paid off, a prepayment penalty applies or the lower payment only comes from a longer term. The calculator’s total-cost figure shows which case you are in.

How do you calculate a refinance for an auto loan?

Work out the monthly payment on your remaining balance at the current APR and months left, and again at the new APR and term. Multiply each payment by its number of months, add fees to the new side, and subtract. The difference is your estimated saving or extra cost.

What is a good rate for refinancing a car?

There is no single good rate; offers depend on your credit, the car and the lender. One benchmark: commercial banks averaged 7.14% on 60-month new-car loans in the second quarter of 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19). Get several written quotes and compare them in the calculator.
